# Collection

## Country Selection and News Source Identification

Countries were selected using purposive sampling stratified by geographic region (seven regions following the UN geoscheme and World Bank regional classifications), World Bank income group (high, upper-middle, lower-middle, and low income), and population size, following conventions established in comparative cross-national media research (Nielsen et al., 2016; Flew, 2018). The Reuters Institute Digital News Report (DNR) 2025 sample of 48 countries served as the base sampling frame, given its established methodology and wide use in the comparative media literature. We then added 22 countries to fill gaps in the region-by-income stratification matrix, prioritising underrepresented regions — particularly Sub-Saharan Africa, the Middle East and North Africa, South and Central Asia, and Eastern Europe — and selecting the highest-population eligible country within each underrepresented cell. Because this procedure excludes microstates (population < 1 million), and because Small Island Developing States (SIDS) face disproportionate climate and health risks central to our analysis, we supplemented the 70-country sample with six additional SIDS spanning multiple geographic regions: Fiji, Guyana, Jamaica, Maldives, Mauritius, and Papua New Guinea. The final sample comprises 75 countries.

For each country, we identified up to five major online newspapers using web traffic and authority metrics — specifically Alexa Traffic Rank, Google PageRank, and Majestic SEO backlink scores — as aggregated by the 4 International Media & Newspapers database (4IMN; https://www.4imn.com/). This approach prioritises outlets with the largest digital readership in each national market. For countries where web traffic data were incomplete or unavailable — predominantly lower-income and smaller-market countries — we supplemented source selection with information from the BBC's country media profiles (http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/country_profiles/default.stm), which provide editorial overviews of major news outlets by country. The resulting corpus draws on up to five news sources per country, with fewer sources available for SIDS and markets with limited digital news infrastructure.

## Data Collection

Scripts for collecting the 2.2M-article corpus across 75 countries. Collection proceeds in two stages:

1. **NewsAPI (≈89% of corpus)** — keyword-based queries in the source language, using the translated keyword set in `../translations/`. Sources indexed by [NewsAPI.ai / Event Registry](https://newsapi.ai/).
2. **ScrapAI (≈11% of corpus)** — custom crawler for sources not indexed by NewsAPI. See https://docs.scrapai.dev/introduction. ScrapAI output lands in its own SQLite (`data/articles.db`) and is then filtered+ingested into `data/climate.db`.

## Pipeline

### NewsAPI path

1. `collect_newsapi_climate.py --year <YEAR>` — query Event Registry for each source × climate keywords; writes `data/newsapi_articles_{year}/{source_uri}_climate.jsonl`. Invoke once per year (2021-2025).
2. `collect_newsapi_health.py --year <YEAR>` — separate second pass for health keywords; writes `data/newsapi_health_{year}/{source_uri}_health.jsonl`. Invoke once per year.
3. `ingest_newsapi_climate.py --year <YEAR>` — ingest the NewsAPI JSONLs into `data/climate.db`.

### ScrapAI path

1. ScrapAI output is stored separately in `data/articles.db` (built outside this repo).
2. `ingest_scrapai_climate.py` — filter articles in `articles.db` by climate keywords (in the article's language + English; word-boundary matching for Latin scripts, substring for CJK/Thai) and insert matches into `data/climate.db`.

### Shared

- `build_source_status.py` — tracks per-source collection status across both paths.

The NewsAPI collectors read `NEWSAPI_KEY` from `.env` and import the `eventregistry` SDK directly.
